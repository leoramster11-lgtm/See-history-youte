=== COLD OPEN ===

Two kilometers down. No sunlight has ever reached this place.
You think you're alone down here. You're not.
Something has been watching you since the second you arrived.
With an eye bigger than a basketball.
The biggest eye of any animal alive.
[pause]
Today, we go down to meet the colossal squid. And we'll find out exactly what would happen to you if you met one.
Hooks that rotate.
A beak that cuts like shears.
Three hearts. Blue blood.
And the one creature on Earth it is truly afraid of.
Nobody has ever filmed an adult one alive in the deep.
So let's go see one.


=== THE DESCENT ===

The Southern Ocean. The sea that circles Antarctica.
The coldest, angriest water on the planet.
Waves here can reach twenty meters. That is a six-story building made of water.
This is your ride.
A steel sphere about two meters wide. Roughly the size of the inside of a small car.
One window. One cable to the surface.
And no way out until they pull you back up.
Descent begins.
[pause]
At thirty meters, the color red is the first thing to die.
Cut your finger here, and your blood looks green.
The water has simply eaten the red part of the light.
At two hundred meters, you enter the twilight zone.
Here, animals start making their own light.
Up to three out of four creatures in the deep can glow.
To lure prey. To scare predators. To find each other in the dark.
Five hundred meters. Two degrees. Water beads on the walls.
Now you can see your own breath.
[pause]
One thousand meters. The last bit of sunlight is gone.
From here all the way to the seafloor, it is night. Forever.
The pressure on the palm of your hand right now? About ten tons. Seven cars, stacked on your hand.
Without the sphere, the air in your lungs would be crushed down to the size of a golf ball.
You wouldn't last a second.
[pause]
The hull groans.
That's normal, you tell yourself. That's just the steel settling under the pressure.
[pause]
It's fine.
What was that?
[pause]
Two thousand meters.
Now the pressure on your palm is twenty tons. Three full-grown African elephants, standing on your hand.
Water colder than the inside of your fridge. And the only thing between you and that pressure is a few centimeters of steel.
Somewhere out here, in the dark, it is waiting.
But first, you need to know what you are dealing with. Because for a hundred years, nobody did.


=== A MONSTER WITH NO FACE ===

1925.
Whalers cut open the stomach of a sperm whale.
Inside, they find two pieces of arm.
But they are not covered in suckers. They are covered in hooks.
Sharp. Curved. And they swivel.
A scientist named Guy Robson describes a brand new species from those two scraps alone.
The colossal squid. But nobody has seen the animal itself.
For decades, all we find are traces.
Beaks in the stomachs of sperm whales. Hundreds of them.
Strange scars on whale skin. Scraps in fishing nets.
Then, February 2007.
A fishing boat is hauling up toothfish from about one and a half kilometers down.
The line goes tight.
On the hook, a fish. And wrapped around the fish, something that refuses to let go.
It takes nearly two hours to bring it to the surface.
When it hits the deck, the crew understands. Nobody has ever seen anything like this.
Four hundred and ninety-five kilograms.
As heavy as a grand piano. As heavy as a big male polar bear. As heavy as seven grown men.
The heaviest invertebrate humans have ever held.
They freeze it whole and ship it to a museum in New Zealand, where it is still on display today.
And the first video of a live colossal squid in the wild? That only came in 2025.
A hundred years after we learned it existed. A baby, thirty centimeters long, clear as glass.
An adult, in its own world, at its own depth? Nobody has ever filmed one.
Until now.


=== FIRST CONTACT ===

You cut the engines.
Silence. Just marine snow, drifting through the light.
Dead plankton. Crumbs of life from the surface. Some of it has been sinking for weeks.
Then, at the very edge of the light, a color.
Dark red. Almost black.
Down here, red light doesn't exist. So red skin looks black. Perfect camouflage.
Only your lamp gives it away.
And then you see it.
The eye.
You can see yourself in it.
Twenty-seven centimeters across, measured on a dead one. Probably even bigger alive. Wider than a dinner plate. Bigger than a basketball.
The lens inside is the size of an orange.
And beside the eyes, small glowing organs. Photophores.
Scientists think it may use them like headlights.
But why would anything need an eye this big, in a place with no light?
Here is what scientists think. When something huge moves through the dark,
it disturbs glowing plankton. And the water lights up all around it.
An eye this size can spot that glow from more than a hundred and twenty meters away. That is longer than a football field.
It's not an eye for hunting.
It's an early-warning system.
It isn't looking for food. It's looking for the thing it fears.
But you are big. You glow. You hum.
And right now, it's deciding what you are.
Your pulse: one hundred forty-two.
Your palms are soaked, even though it's five degrees in here.
You hold your breath. You can't help feeling you're breathing too loud.
It can't hear you.
Can it?
Then it moves. And it just keeps going. And going.
Where did it go?
Let's freeze this moment, and take a proper look at it.


=== ANATOMY OF A MONSTER ===

Here it is, next to you.
Weight: up to around half a ton.
Picture a horse. Now give it eight arms, two tentacles, and a beak.
And the biggest ones out there could be heavier still. Nobody knows.
Length: up to about ten meters, by most estimates.
Almost as long as a city bus. Stand it on its tail, and it would look into the windows of a three-story building.
Its main body, the mantle, is about two and a half meters. Taller than any doorway in your house.
Its cousin, the giant squid, is longer and thinner. But the colossal is heavier. A lot heavier.
Eight arms. Two long hunting tentacles.
And at the end of each tentacle, a club, armed with hooks.
Each hook swivels on its base.
When prey pulls away, the hook doesn't rip out. It turns, and digs in deeper.
No other squid on Earth has weapons like this.
Now, look between the arms.
The beak.
The largest beak of any squid.
Hard as horn, with edges that work like a pair of metal shears.
That's why beaks are what we usually find. Whales can digest everything else. Not the beak.
Inside, it gets even stranger.
One heart.
Two hearts.
Three.
Two push blood through the gills. The third drives it through the body.
And the blood is blue. Built on copper instead of iron.
Its brain is shaped like a donut, wrapped around its throat.
Swallow something too big, and the food would press against its own brain.
So it slices every meal into small pieces first.
And here is the strangest number of all.
Scientists estimate a half-ton colossal squid needs only about thirty grams of food a day. Roughly one slice of bread.
It doesn't chase. It waits. Still, for hours. Saving every drop of energy.
It isn't a hunter.
It's an ambush.
And you are hanging right in front of it.


=== THE ATTACK ===

Nothing happens.
One second. Ten.
A full minute.
You decide to angle the lamp away. Maybe the light is bothering it.
[pause]
Strike!
The tentacles fire in a fraction of a second. Hooks rake across the steel.
Eight arms close around the sphere.
Suckers. Hooks. Red flesh pressed flat against the glass.
It's pulling you in. Toward the beak.
Half a ton of muscle against two tons of steel.
So, what would actually happen to you?
Two scenarios.
Inside the sphere, you survive. Steel is the one thing it cannot cut.
But you're hanging from a single cable, and it's starting to strain.
Now imagine you are outside.
Honestly? The pressure would kill you long before it ever touched you.
But say some impossible suit keeps you alive.
The hooks sink in. And turn.
The arms drag you in, toward the beak.
And the beak doesn't bite. It slices. Piece by piece. Small enough to fit through that donut-shaped brain.
Slowly. In total darkness. Two kilometers from the nearest human being.
[pause]
And then, it freezes.
It's not looking at you anymore.
It's seen something.


=== THE ONE IT FEARS ===

[pause]
Click. You don't hear it with your ears.
You feel it in your chest.
Click. Click. Click-click-click-click.
A sperm whale. Hunting.
It sees with sound. Echolocation.
Its clicks are among the loudest sounds made by any animal on Earth. Around two hundred and thirty decibels underwater.
Remember the glowing plankton? There it is.
This is what that eye was built for. This is what it has been watching for its whole life.
It lets go of you.
It runs.
Too late.
A bull sperm whale.
Fifty tons. As heavy as eight African elephants.
Eighteen meters long. Stand it up, and it reaches the top of a six-story building.
It dove here from the surface on a single breath. It can stay down for more than an hour.
And inside that giant head is the largest brain of any animal that has ever lived. About five times heavier than yours.
Now look at its skin.
Rings left by suckers.
Long gouges from hooks.
Every single one of them, a fight with something like the animal next to you.
The squid strikes first. Hooks, straight into the whale's face.
It sprays a cloud of ink.
Useless. The whale sees with sound, not light.
Fifty thousand kilos, against five hundred.
The hooks leave fresh scars. But the whale doesn't stop.
It only has teeth in its lower jaw. It doesn't chew.
It swallows.
[pause]


=== THE ASCENT ===

When the ink clears, there is nothing left.
Years from now, someone might find one more beak in the stomach of a whale.
And that's all that will be left of the monster you saw.
They pull you up. Two hours to the surface. You don't say a word.
Somewhere below you, there could be thousands of them.
We don't know how many there are. How long they live. How big they can really get.
We have better maps of the surface of Mars than of our own ocean floor.
The colossal squid isn't hiding from us. It just lives where we never go.
Next time, we go deeper. Down to where light isn't safety. Light is a trap.
Subscribe. We're only getting started.
